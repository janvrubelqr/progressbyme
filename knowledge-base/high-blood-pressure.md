# High blood pressure

## Key facts

**The numbers**
- Systolic (top number) = pressure while the heart beats. Diastolic (bottom) = pressure between beats.
- Categories (ACC/AHA 2017 guideline):
  - Normal: systolic <120 **and** diastolic <80
  - Elevated: 120–129 **and** <80
  - Stage 1 hypertension: 130–139 **or** 80–89
  - Stage 2 hypertension: ≥140 **or** ≥90
  - Hypertensive crisis: >180 **and/or** >120 — seek care immediately if paired with symptoms (severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, confusion, vision changes)
- Cardiovascular risk starts climbing as low as 115/75 and roughly doubles for every 20-point rise in systolic or 10-point rise in diastolic.
- Diagnosis should be based on the *average* of at least two readings, ideally on separate occasions.

**Measuring it correctly** (a wrong technique can skew the reading by up to 10%)
- No caffeine or smoking in the 30 minutes before.
- Sit quietly for 5 minutes first, feet on the floor, don't talk during the measurement.
- Arm supported on a table/desk with the cuff at heart level (a dangling or lap-supported arm reads falsely high).
- Cuff on bare skin, covering at least 80% of the upper arm — a too-small cuff also reads high.
- Take it in both arms at least once; use the higher one for treatment decisions.

**Other types worth knowing**
- White-coat hypertension: high only at the doctor's office, normal elsewhere.
- Masked hypertension: normal at the doctor's office, high elsewhere (still raises cardiovascular risk).
- Labile hypertension: swings unusually widely through the day (e.g. caffeine, anxiety, stress).
- Orthostatic hypotension: BP drops sharply on standing (systolic ↓>20 or diastolic ↓>10 within 1–3 min of standing) — more common over 65.

**What actually moves the number** (potential systolic reduction, from clinical guidelines)
- Losing weight: ~5–20 mmHg per 22 lb (≈10 kg) lost
- DASH diet: ~11 mmHg
- Cutting sodium by ~1,000 mg/day: ~5–6 mmHg
- More potassium (3,500–5,000 mg/day, from food not pills): ~4–5 mmHg
- 30 min/day moderate aerobic exercise: ~4–8 mmHg
- Limiting alcohol (≤2 drinks/day men, ≤1 women): ~4 mmHg
- Quitting smoking: ~2–8 mmHg
- Isometric exercise (wall squats, planks — muscles tense without moving) appears to be the single most effective *type* of strength training specifically for lowering blood pressure.

**Sodium/potassium**
- Recommended sodium cap: 2,300 mg/day generally, 1,500 mg/day if hypertensive. Average American intake: ~3,400 mg/day.
- ~75% of dietary sodium comes from processed/restaurant food, not the salt shaker.
- Top 10 US sodium sources: breads/rolls, pizza, sandwiches, cold cuts, soups, burritos/tacos, savory snacks, chicken, cheese, eggs/omelets.
- Potassium-based salt substitutes are risky for people on ACE inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics, or with kidney disease — don't recommend broadly.

**Other levers**
- Caffeine: limit to <300 mg/day (~3 cups coffee); raises BP mainly in people unaccustomed to it.
- Alcohol: newer evidence suggests even ~1 standard drink/day (12g alcohol) measurably raises hypertension risk.
- Many common drugs raise BP: NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen), decongestants (pseudoephedrine), some antidepressants, oral corticosteroids, oral contraceptives.
- Meditation / the "relaxation response" (10–20 min, 2x/day) has real, if modest, blood-pressure-lowering evidence.
